## 1. Machines, implements and attachments
Format: components / motions to animate / cutaway candidates / how it connects to the tractor.

### Land clearing and forming (AGRICULTURAL_MACHINERIES §11)
- **Hand tools:** axe, matchet, brush hook, chain saw (up to 1.5 m bar, two-stroke).
- **Bulldozer:** front blade on a crawler. Knocks vegetation over but takes topsoil with it; hauls soil up to 25 m in land forming.
- **Rake:** a dozer with a slatted, toothed blade, so soil falls through the slats. Best in sandy soils.
- **Tree pusher:** a frame on the front of a tractor or dozer that adds leverage to push trees over and uproot them.
- **Chaining:** a heavy chain slung between two crawlers, one running along the edge and one through the bush. Effective for trees up to 45 cm diameter and up to 2500 stems/ha.
- **Root plough:** undercuts roots at 20–50 cm depth and shatters surface crusts.
- **Shear blade:** sharp edge plus a "stinger" wedge that splits the tree first. A hydraulic scissor-type version cuts softwood up to 76 cm.
- **Rotary mower:** PTO-driven horizontal blades for stems up to 10 cm.
- **Land forming:** scraper (bowl, cutting edge, cover; lifts, carries, dumps), grade sloper, land plane (long frame with an adjustable central blade), V-ditcher. A mouldboard plough or ridger can make small channels.
- **Tree count:** done on a 100 m × 10 m strip.
- **Also in the marking guides:** bale loader and tree extractor.

### Primary tillage (§12)
- **Mouldboard plough**
  - Parts: share (point, gunnel, wing; types bar, shin, slip, slip-nose), mouldboard (stubble, general-purpose, slatted), landside with detachable heel, frog (joins share, mouldboard and landside), standard, beam, handles (animal-drawn only), brace and shin (from the 2018/19 guide sketch).
  - Attachments: coulter (disc or knife, centre behind the share point) and jointer (mini-mouldboard).
  - "Suction" clearances (vertical and horizontal) of 0.48–1.25 cm hold it in the ground.
  - Motion to animate: the furrow slice being cut and inverted.
  - Reversible (two-way) plough: left-hand bottoms mounted above right-hand ones, rotated by a trip lever or hydraulics at the headland.
- **Disc plough**
  - Parts: concave discs on a frame, standards, disc brackets, scrapers, furrow (rear) wheel with spring, cross shaft, top-link connection, frame extension (2018/19 sketch).
  - Penetrates by its own weight (heavier build, added weights).
  - Tilt angle (disc face to vertical) is 15–25°; disc angle (face to travel direction) is 42–45°.
  - Preferred for hard, dry or sticky soils.
- **Chisel plough** (deep tines that break the subsoil) and **subsoiler** are listed only.
- **Hitching a plough:** mounted (3-point linkage), semi-mounted (front on the lower links or drawbar, rear on a castor wheel, optional remote ram) or trailed (drawbar).
  - Centre of draft: about 5 cm from the landside at the top of the share.
  - Centre of power: mid-drawbar.
  - Line of draft: should be straight and parallel to the direction of travel.
  - A depth wheel raised gives deeper work.
  - Draft/traction control: soil load compresses the top link, which signals the hydraulic lift.
  - The cranked cross shaft and crank screw set furrow width; top-link length sets pitch.
- **Ploughing patterns** (good for animation): in lands (headland then plots), round-and-round, one-way (reversible).

### Secondary tillage
- **Cultivators**
  - Rigid tine: shovel or sweep, staggered tines, reversible points.
  - Spring tine: shock-absorbing, shallower working depth.
  - Depth is set by the shovel position, top-link pitch, land wheel or hydraulics.
- **Harrows** (disc, spike-tooth, chain), rotary cultivators and rollers are listed only.
- **Disc harrow and disc ridger:** sketches only, in the 2018/19 guide (gang frames, scalloped discs; ridger is a toolbar with two opposed discs on adjustable standards).

### Planting (§13)
- **Seed drill**
  - Parts: seed box, fluted-roller metering (rate chart on the shaft), agitator, seed tubes, boot, furrow openers, covering device, depth-regulating lever, frame, ground-wheel drive by chain-and-sprocket or V-belt.
  - Size is stated as openers × spacing, e.g. 3 × 200 mm.
- **Planter:** cell or pocket seed plate turning in the hopper, sprocket/gear ratio from the drive wheel, furrow opener, boot, seed tube, coverer. Good cutaway: the rotating seed plate dropping seeds.
- **Jab planter:** single or rotary. Jaws stay closed by gravity and are opened by ground pressure to drop the seed.
- **Root-crop planters:** direct-drop with a bell rung by the drive wheel; semi-automatic with a revolving ring or belt of cups.
- **Seed-cum-fertilizer drill:** split box; seed placed 2–3 cm from the fertilizer.

### Fertilizer equipment (§14)
- **Manure spreader**
  - Parts: box with an endless double chain-and-slat apron (driven by ratchet and pawl, so apron speed sets the rate), lower and upper toothed beaters (lower one counter-rotates relative to the wheels at 6–7× wheel speed), spiral auger spreader chain-driven from the beater shaft.
  - Driven by the ground wheels or the PTO.
- **Tractor front loader:** hydraulic bucket.
- **Liquid-manure tanker:** 800–1200 L, trailed, with splash plate or rotating disc.
- **Spinning-disc broadcaster**
  - Parts: cone hopper (plastic or fibreglass), agitator, adjustable shutter/feed gate, PTO-driven disc(s).
  - Spread width is about 6–9 m.
  - Rate depends on gate opening, forward speed and disc speed.
- **Anhydrous ammonia rig:** tank, self-compensating metering valve, applicator blade/tube; injected 10–15 cm deep (82% N).
- **Low-pressure liquid applicator:** knife tines, flow through an orifice.

### Crop protection (§15)
- **Duster:** hopper, agitator, metering orifice, blower. Shoulder-, belly- or PTO-mounted.
- **Sprayer**
  - Parts: tank, pump, nozzle, pressure-relief valve, gauge, boom or lance, cut-off valve, filters (tank, pre-pump, nozzle).
  - Pump types: piston/plunger with air chamber, gear/rotary, centrifugal, diaphragm.
- **Nozzle cutaway:** cap, gasket, orifice disc, whirl plate with slanted holes.
  - Fan nozzle: no whirl plate.
  - Hollow cone: whirl plate without a centre hole.
  - Solid cone: whirl plate with a centre hole.
- **Classes:** knapsack (hand or foot pump), mist blower, tractor boom sprayer (PTO pump), self-propelled, aircraft.
- **ULV/CDA sprayer:** 11 W motor spinning serrated discs at 7000–9000 rpm, bottle reservoir, colour-coded nozzle, handle holding 8 or 16 batteries.
- **Bird scarer:** acetylene or electronic bang.

### Forage (§16)
- **Cutter-bar mower**
  - Parts: ledger plates, knife (sickle) sections, knife clips, wear plates, inner and outer shoes (the outer shoe is the crop divider).
  - Drive: PTO through gears to a crank and pitman, converting rotation to reciprocation. Prime animation and cutaway.
  - Adjustments: registration, and alignment with a lead of 6 mm per metre of bar.
  - An animal-drawn version takes drive from a ground wheel.
- **Rotary mower:** horizontal blades.
- **Hay handling:** rakes, swath turners (rake-bar with pegs; finger-wheel with 4–6 angled spring-tine wheels), roller crusher/conditioner.
- **Baler**
  - Parts: pick-up reel tines, auger, packer arms ("like connecting rods"), bale chamber, reciprocating ram ("like a piston") with ram knife, knotter. Bale is about 35 × 45 × 90 cm.
  - Driven by the PTO (the PTO doc mentions a 540 rpm baler).
- **Forage harvester combine:** cylinder or flail type; cuts, chops and loads.

### Cereal harvesting and threshing (§17–19)
- **Reaper:** mower plus collection platform, with a manual or mechanical rake.
- **Binder:** adds a knotter.
- **Thresher**
  - Parts: cylinder (spike or rasp-bar), slotted concave, feed hopper, fan or aspirator, reciprocating sieves.
  - Pedal thresher: crank-driven toothed drum at about 500 rpm.
  - Groundnut thresher: peg-tooth cylinder, slotted concave, blower.
  - Decorticator: semi-rotary (oscillating pegged beater in a slotted trough) or rotary.
  - Maize sheller: IAR hand cone with serrated ribs; pegged rotating disc with a spring-set gap; pegged cylinder with perforated concave.
  - Winnower: hopper, fan, sieves.
- **Combine**
  - Flow: cutter bar → auger or belt conveyor → cylinder and concave → straw walkers, grain pan, sieves, fan → grain auger with tailings return → tank or bagging. Size is the cutter-bar width.
  - Maize head: snapping rolls; size given as number of rows.
  - Maize picker-sheller: gathering chains, snapping rolls, husking rolls with rubber insets, shelling cylinder with lugs inside a bar cage.
  - The combine is a strong candidate for an animated crop-flow cutaway.

### Root crops (§20)
- **Common components:** digging blade, inclined elevator-shaker (slats or rods, adjustable by sprocket), disc coulters, agitators (sprockets on the chain), depth wheels (manual or hydraulic), overload clutch.
- **Groundnut:** digger, digger-shaker, digger-shaker-inverter, groundnut combine (picks up the windrow), digger plus clod crusher.
- **Cassava digger-elevator:** wedge with two side discs on drums, works to 300 mm. The bar elevator runs faster than forward speed to strip soil. Recovers about 85%.
- **Manual cassava lifter:** tongs and lever; about 400 N input gives about 1500 N lift.
- **Potato:** digger-elevator-shaker, and a spinner (tines rotating at right angles to travel).
- **Tool-bars:** Ariana, Emcot, Arara.

### Irrigation and processing (§21–22)
- **Water lifting:** shadoof (bucket, pole, pivot, counterweight), swing basket, Archimedean screw, Persian wheel (gear plus bucket chain), chain pump (discs in a tube).
- **Pump cutaways:** reciprocating piston pump (inlet and delivery valves); centrifugal (impeller, volute vs turbine/diffuser casing; multistage); propeller; jet (nozzle plus venturi); diaphragm; submersible; foot valve for priming.
- **Mills:** burr/plate mill (grooved plates, handwheel gap, spring), hammer mill (high-speed hammers, screen, aspirator), roller crusher for sugarcane.
- **Handling:** mixer (vertical auger in a cone hopper); conveyors (belt, chain-flight, auger, pneumatic); hydraulic tipping trailer.

## 2. Tractor subsystems

### Types and evolution (Tractor_as_a_versatile_equipment; all 3 marking guides)
- **The 12 types:**
  - Crawler (steel tracks in the 1930s; rubber-belt Challenger in the 1990s).
  - Standard (CG about one-third of the wheelbase ahead of the rear axle).
  - Row-crop.
  - High-clearance (cane).
  - Utility (front loader).
  - Orchard (lower, no ROPS).
  - Lawn and garden (under 15 kW).
  - Power tiller (two-wheel, rotary tiller, 5–12 kW single-cylinder engine).
  - Tree skidder.
  - Skid-steer.
  - 4WD with smaller front wheels.
  - 4WD with equal wheels and articulated frame (100–300+ kW).
- **Modern features:** power steering, epicyclic front hubs, wet disc brakes, power-shift transmission, ROPS cab.
- **Attachment modes:** towed single-point, mounted 3-point, semi-mounted, frame-mounted. Power goes out by PTO or hydraulic hoses.
- **History:** 1900 California giant (5 m wide × 3 m diameter wheels, 36 t), Winnipeg trials 1908–12, Nebraska Test Law 1919.

### Hitching (Hitching_and_Mechanics_of_Tractor_Chassis / Macmillan Ch. 6)
- **Trailed hitch:**
  - Fig 6.1a: no vertical force.
  - Fig 6.1b: unbalanced trailer, adds vertical force.
- **Semi-mounted (Fig 6.2):** pivots on the lower links, rear castor wheel. Free to pivot vertically, rigid in plan; used for ploughs and offset mowers.
- **Fully mounted 3-point (Figs 6.3–6.4):** a four-bar chain (lower links, top link, implement mast, chassis).
  - Virtual hitch point V is where the top and lower link lines meet; it is also the instantaneous centre of rotation.
  - Fig 6.5 stages:
    - (a) Penetration: V is below ground and the draft line passes above it, so the implement is pulled in.
    - (b) Free-link equilibrium: the draft line passes through V.
    - (c) Restrained working: the draft line passes above V.
- **Weight-transfer hitch for trailed implements (Fig 6.15):** rigid link XYZ on the 3-point linkage, chain ZG to the trailer drawbar, lift force F held constant by a hydraulic valve.
- **PTO-driven trailer (Fig 6.14)** and **two-wheel walking tractor (Fig 6.13)** with handle force U and an adjustable front counterweight.

### Hydraulics (2016/17 and 2017/18 marking guides)
- **Lift system:** sump/tank, pump, lever-operated control valve, relief valve, piston and cylinder, cross shaft, lift rods, lift arms. Lift position raises the implement; lower position sets depth.
- **Vane pump:** slotted rotor, radially sliding vanes, rotor (cam) ring, inlet and outlet.
- **Double-acting cylinder:** barrel, piston, seals, rod, two oil ports, clevis eyes.
- **Pump vs motor table:** for a pump, actual delivery is less than theoretical and actual torque more than theoretical; the reverse for a motor.
- **System components:** pump, motor, valves, lines, coolers, sump, accumulator, controls, actuators, filters.
- **Fluid:** viscosity 13–54 mm²/s; pour point 11 °C below the lowest operating temperature.

### PTO (Power_Take_off_shaft; marking guides)
- **Speeds:** 540 rpm (6-spline, all sizes) and 1000 rpm (larger equipment).
- **Driveline:** the implement driveline (telescoping shaft with U-joints) runs from the tractor PTO stub to the implement.
- **Guards:** tractor master shield, shaft shield, connector shields, plastic bearing bushings that hold the shield.
- **Uses:** generator, grinder, grain auger, baler, mower, water pump, processing.
- **Safety fact:** a 540 rpm shaft wraps 7 ft of hood string in 1 s.

### Hooke's joint (Hooke_s_joint_1, Hooke's joint analysis-2 images, DOC-20210628-WA0047)
- **Components:** two U-yokes (forks) and a cross (spider), needle-roller bearing cups, circlips, grease nipple and seals.
- **Double cardan:** two joints joined by a centre yoke with a ball-and-socket centring joint.
- **Propeller shaft:** steel, aluminium or composite tube with welded yokes, slip (sleeve) yoke, pinion flange yoke, balance weights, cardboard liner.
- **Characteristics:** speed varies twice per revolution; joint phasing; cancelling angles.
- **Advantages:** high torque, long bearing life, high angle, shock capacity.
- **Disadvantages:** non-uniform output speed, so inertia torque; not for high speed.
- **Constant-velocity joints with labelled cutaways:** Tracta, Rzeppa/Birfield ball-and-cage, Weiss/Bendix four-ball, tripode (tripod pot) joint, rubber/flexible couplings.

### Power measurement (Tractor_power_measurement)
- **Definitions:** brake, PTO, drawbar, friction, observed and corrected power.
- **Dynamometer essentials:** control torque, measure torque, measure speed, dissipate power.
- **Dynamometer types:**
  - Prony brake: rotor, brake shoes, lever arm, scale, tachometer.
  - Hydraulic (water): cradled case with torque arm.
  - Cradled DC electric: 0.25% accuracy.
  - Eddy-current, air-brake.
  - Shop-type: pressure gauge on the torque arm.
  - Spring drawbar.
  - Strain-gauge torque meter.
- **Fuel flow:** rotameter with a tapered tube and float.
- **Test standards:** OECD compulsory test codes: main PTO, hydraulic lift, drawbar ballasted, turning, CG position, braking, noise.

## 3. Equations for simulation

### Hitching / chassis (Macmillan Ch. 6, also in the Hitching docx)
Variables:
- α = slope angle; θ = angle of implement force P (or soil force S) to the ground.
- W = tractor weight; Wf, Wr = static front/rear wheel weights; Vf, Vr = dynamic weights.
- x = wheelbase; xr, xf = CG distance from rear/front axle; yg = CG height above the rear axle.
- r = rear rolling radius; x' = rear contact point to drawbar; y' = drawbar height; y = r − y'.
- M = wheel torque = H·r; H = tractive force; ψ' = gross tractive coefficient; ρ = rolling-resistance coefficient.

Equations:
- **Centre of gravity**
  - Eq 6.1: xr = Wf·x / W
  - Eq 6.2: x'r = W'f·x'' / W (front raised)
  - Eq 6.3: yg = (xr − x'r/cosβ) / tanβ, with β = atan((rr − rf)/x) + atan((y' − rr)/x'')
  - Rule of thumb: xr ≈ 30% of x.
- **Weight transfer**
  - Eq 6.4: Vf = Wf − W sinα (r+yg)/x − P cosθ·y'/x − P sinθ·x'/x
  - Eq 6.5: Vr = Wr + W sinα (r+yg)/x + P cosθ·y'/x + P sinθ (x+x')/x
  - Eq 6.6–6.7: same for downhill with a forward/downward push.
  - Instability is reached when Vf = 0.
- **Tractive coefficient with rolling resistance**
  - Eq 6.8: ψ' = [W sinα + P cosθ + ρ(W cosα + P sinθ)] / Vr
  - Eq 6.9: maximum slope uphill, tanα_u = (ψ'xf − ρx) / (x − ψ'(r+yg))
  - Eq 6.10: maximum slope downhill, tanα_d = (ψ'xf + ρx) / (x + ψ'(r+yg))
- **Hitch comparison** with the same soil force S; a = implement wheel distance, b = horizontal position of the soil force, z = vertical offset of the soil force:
  - Trailed (Eq 6.11–6.12): Vr = Wr + S cosθ·y'/x
  - Semi-mounted (Eq 6.13): Vr = Wr + S sinθ (a−b)(x+x')/(a·x) + S cosθ [a·y' + (z+y')(x+x')]/(a·x)
  - Fully mounted (Eq 6.15–6.16): Vr = Wr + S sinθ (x+x'+b)/x − S cosθ·z/x; Vf = Wf − S sinθ (x'+b)/x + S cosθ·z/x
- **Weight-transfer conditions**
  - Rear gains weight when tanθ > z/(x+x'+b) (Eq 6.17).
  - Front loses weight when tanθ > z/(x'+b) (Eq 6.18).
  - Mounted beats semi-mounted when tanθ > (z+y')/b (Eq 6.19).
  - Mounted beats trailed when tanθ > (z+y')/(x+x'+b) (Eq 6.20).
  - Semi-mounted beats trailed when tanθ > (z+y')/(a−b) (Eq 6.21).
  - Summarised in Tables 6.1–6.2 and Fig 6.12.
- **Walking tractor** (handle at xh)
  - Eq 6.22: U0 = W cosα·xr/xh − W sinα (r+yg)/xh
  - Eq 6.23–6.24: U = U0 − P cosθ·y'/xh − P sinθ·x'/xh
  - Balance condition: xr = ψ'·y'
- **PTO trailer** (Eq 6.26)
  - Pulled: P = W' sinα
  - Driven: ψ' = a·tanα / ((r+yt)·tanα + b)
- **Weight-transfer hitch**
  - T = W'(a−b)/a
  - Eq 6.30: Vr = Wr + W'(a−b)(x+x')/(a·x) + P·y'(a+x+x')/(a·x) + F·c(a+x+x')/(a·x)
  - Eq 6.31: Vf = Wf − W'(a−b)x'/(a·x) − P(x'+a)y'/(a·x) − F·c(a+x')/(a·x)
- **Impending instability**
  - Eq 6.35: ψ' = tanα + (tanαs − tanα)/h
  - tanαs = xr/(r+yg); αs is about 40°, tanαs 0.6–0.8.
  - h = y'/(r+yg); 0.6 is typical, 0.7–0.8 is dangerous.
  - Worked case at tanα = 0.3: ψ' = 0.72 (h = 0.7) and 0.67 (h = 0.8).
- **Drawbar height that tips the tractor (Problem 6.16):** y' = W·xr / (A·c + W·tanφ) = 0.85 m, with A = 0.076 m², c = 2 kPa, φ = 32°.

Reference data and worked answers:
- **Farmland tractor (Macmillan App. II):**
  - Weights: W = 2850 kg (27.9 kN); Wr = 2030 kg (19.9 kN); Wf = 820 kg (8.0 kN).
  - Dimensions: x = 1.88 m; xr = 0.54; xf = 1.34; yg = 0.13; x' = 0.60; y' = 0.45; y = 0.185.
  - Rear tyres: r = 0.635 m on 14.9×28.
- **Problem 6.6:** rear spray tank, 60 kg empty, CG 1.5 m behind the axle and 1.0 m high.
  - Answers: Vf = 5.92 kN; 164 kg of water allowed at ψ' = 0.20 up 10°; 9.48 kN and ψ' = −0.27 down 10°.
- **Problem 6.8:** 15° slope, pull angle 10°, ψ' = 0.8 gives P = 17.1 kN and Vr = 30.1 kN. It needs iteration.
- **Problem 6.9:** ρ = 0.025 (bitumen), 0.05 (firm) and 0.1 (ploughed soil), with loads of 100, 300 and 500 kg.

### Hooke's joint (2016/17 and 2017/18 guides; analysis-2 images; Dunn)
- **Velocity ratio:** ωB/ωA = cosδ / (1 − sin²δ·cos²θ), where δ = shaft angle and θ = driver rotation angle.
  - (ωB)max = ωA/cosδ at θ = 0° or 180°.
  - (ωB)min = ωA·cosδ at θ = 90° or 270°.
- **Angular acceleration:** αB = −ωA²·cosδ·sin²δ·sin2θ / (1 − cos²θ·sin²δ)². Inertia torque = I·αB.
- **Double joint:** with equal angles and the intermediate yokes in the same plane, ωB = ωA.
- **Exam example:** δ = 20°, 1000 rpm, θ = 10°…90° gives 1060, 1048, 1030, 1009, 987, 968, 953, 943, 940 rpm. A second joint at 15° gives 1095…1024 rpm.
- **Inertia example:** 1000 rpm, 20°, θ = 45°, weight 147 N, k = 0.25 m.
  - Speed range: 939.7–1064 rpm.
  - αB = −1365 rad/s².
  - Inertia torque 1278.4 N·m; driver torque 1276.2 N·m.
- **Dunn's 45° example:** maximum ratio √2, minimum 1/√2.
- **Speed-curve figure:** a 30° joint swings about 850–1150 rpm.

### Hydraulics (2016/17 and 2017/18 guides)
- **Pump formulas:**
  - Theoretical delivery: QT = D·N/1000 (L/min, with D in cm³/rev).
  - Volumetric efficiency: ev = QA/QT.
  - Theoretical torque: TT = ΔP·D/2π (N·m, with ΔP in MPa).
  - Hydraulic power: Ph = QA·ΔP/60 (kW).
  - Overall efficiency: ep = ev·eT; shaft power Ps = Ph/ep.
- **Pump example:** 1800 rpm, 18 MPa, ev = 0.8, eT = 0.9.
  - TT = 429.7 N·m, TA = 477.5 N·m.
  - Ph = 64.8 kW, Ps = 90 kW, heat loss 25.2 kW.
- **Cylinder:** F = P·A, v = Q/A, retract area = A − A_rod.
- **Cylinder example:** 7.62 cm bore, 2.5 cm rod, 17.2 MPa, 68.13 L/min.
  - Forces: 78.4 kN extend, 70.0 kN retract.
  - Speeds: 0.249 m/s extend, 0.279 m/s retract.
  - Return flow 76.3 L/min.

### Power measurement (Tractor_power_measurement)
- **Prony brake:** P = 2π·L·F·n/60000 kW = 2π·n·T/60000.
- **Hydraulic dynamometer capacity:** proportional to N³·D⁵.
- **Atmospheric correction:**
  - ρs/ρo = (BPs/BPo)(To/Ts)
  - fa = (BPs/BPo)(To/Ts)^n
  - Pbs = fa·Pbo + Pf(fa − 1), usually simplified to fa·Pbo.
- **Standard conditions:** the notes use 99 kPa and 298 K; the marking guide uses 101.3 kPa and 15.5 °C.

### Field machinery (AGRICULTURAL_MACHINERIES)
- **Plough draft:** draft = unit draft × furrow cross-section. Unit draft is about 1 N/cm² in sand and 2 N/cm² in clay. Power = draft × speed; metric hp = kgf·m/s ÷ 75.
  - Error in the notes: the worked example (150 mm × 60 mm, 180 N at 1 m/s) gives "2.4 hp" by treating N as kg. The correct answer is about 0.245 hp.
  - Error in the 2018/19 guide: the two-bottom 150 mm, 70 mm, 2.8 km/h, 2 N/cm² question uses only one bottom. Correct is 210 cm², 420 N, about 327 W.
- **Seed drill calibration:** N = L/(πD), with L = 10000·A_ha/W.
  - Example: 5×150 mm, 80 kg/ha, D = 1 m gives 0.75 m width, 1333 m and 425 revolutions per 0.1 ha, 1.6 kg per tube.
- **Sprayer**
  - Flow is proportional to √pressure.
  - Doubling speed (4 to 8 km/h) halves the application rate.
  - Boom calibration: L = 1000/W for 0.1 ha. Example: 10 nozzles at 200 mm need 2500 m for 0.5 ha.
- **ULV sprayer:** 70 µm droplets; 1 L/ha gives 50 droplets/cm²; under 5 L/ha vs 200+ L/ha conventional; 1 m/s × 10 m swath = 3.6 ha/h; wind 1–20 km/h.
- **Spinner calibration:** L = 400/W for 0.04 ha, then multiply by 25.
- **Mower lead:** 6 mm per metre of cutter bar.
- **Hay moisture:** about 80% at cutting, dried to 20% for storage.
